About Mahdia

Mahdia is a coastal city in northern Tunisia, known for its rich history as the capital of the Fatimid Caliphate in the 10th century. Founded in 921 AD by Caliph Abdallah al-Mahdi, the city was built on a narrow peninsula jutting into the Mediterranean Sea, giving it a naturally defensive position and stunning sea views.

Today, Mahdia is celebrated for its pristine white-sand beaches, authentic medina, vibrant fishing port, and centuries-old monuments that tell the story of Islamic civilization in North Africa. It's a quieter alternative to Tunisia's more tourist-heavy resorts, offering a genuine glimpse into Tunisian coastal life.

History

Historical Timeline

921 AD

Founding of Mahdia

Caliph Abdallah al-Mahdi Billah establishes Mahdia as the capital of the Fatimid Caliphate. The city is built on a strategic peninsula.

10th Century

Fatimid Golden Age

Mahdia flourishes as a center of power, trade, and Islamic learning. The Great Mosque of Mahdia is built during this period.

1087

Norman Invasion

The Norman fleet from Sicily attacks Mahdia. The city is temporarily occupied before being reclaimed by the Zirids.

16th Century

Ottoman Era

Mahdia becomes part of the Ottoman Empire. The fortifications of Borj el-Kebir (the Great Fort) are strengthened.

1956

Modern Tunisia

Following Tunisia's independence from France, Mahdia grows as a center for fishing, olive oil production, and tourism.

Culture & Traditions

🏝

Medina & Souks

Wander through the narrow streets of the old medina, where artisans sell traditional pottery, textiles, and jewelry in the bustling souks.

🍲

Cuisine

Mahdia is famous for its fresh seafood, couscous, brik (stuffed pastry), and harissa. The local olive oil is among the finest in Tunisia.

🎶

Stambali Music

A spiritual music tradition rooted in sub-Saharan African heritage, Stambali ceremonies feature hypnotic rhythms and colorful costumes.

🏙

Festivals

The annual Festival of the Sea celebrates Mahdia's maritime heritage with boat races, music performances, and traditional dance.
